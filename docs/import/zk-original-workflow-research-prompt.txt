Your objective is to reconstruct, as accurately and comprehensively as possible, how Luhmann actually built, organized, maintained, and used his Zettelkasten.

PRIORITY: PRIMARY SOURCES AND MACHINE-READABLE DATA

Start with these resources, prioritizing structured data, searchable transcriptions, JSON, XML, and HTML over scanned documents and secondary summaries.

1. NIKLAS LUHMANN ARCHIVE
   https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand

   This is the authoritative archival source for Luhmann's
   Zettelkasten, manuscripts, bibliography, and related materials.

   Explore the digital Zettelkasten collection, including:
   - Individual note transcriptions.
   - Original note identifiers and addresses.
   - Folgezettel sequences.
   - Cross-references.
   - Bibliographic references.
   - Indexes and organizational metadata.
   - Relationships between notes.

2. NIKLAS LUHMANN ARCHIVE API AND DOCUMENTATION
   https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/projekt/dokumentation

   Investigate the API and its machine-readable data formats.
   Prefer JSON and TEI-XML wherever available.

   Preserve original identifiers, references, addresses,
   metadata, and relationships. Do not flatten the collection
   into an unstructured collection of text documents.

3. LUHMANN'S 1968 MANUSCRIPT:
   TECHNIK DES ZETTELKASTENS

   https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/manuskripte/manuskript/MS_2906_0004

   Use the archival transcription and machine-readable versions
   where available.

   Treat this as a primary source describing the construction
   and operation of his system.

4. LUHMANN'S 1981 ESSAY:
   COMMUNICATING WITH SLIP BOXES

   English translation:
   https://luhmann.surge.sh/communicating-with-slip-boxes

   Analyze Luhmann's own explanation of the intellectual and
   operational role of his Zettelkasten.

5. ENGLISH TRANSLATIONS OF LUHMANN'S NOTES

   https://zettelkasten.de/posts/luhmanns-zettel-translated/

   Examine the translated notes as supplementary primary-source
   material. Preserve their original note references where
   possible.

6. JOHANNES F. K. SCHMIDT (2015):
   DER ZETTELKASTEN NIKLAS LUHMANNS ALS
   ÜBERRASCHUNGSGENERATOR

   https://www.uni-bielefeld.de/soz/luhmann-archiv/pdf/jschmidt_zettelkasten-als-uberraschungsgenerator.pdf

   Use this scholarly analysis to understand the actual
   organization and operation of Luhmann's collection.

   This is a secondary source. Distinguish Schmidt's
   interpretations from Luhmann's own statements and practices.

7. SUPPLEMENTARY MACHINE-READABLE DATASET

   https://github.com/martijnaslander/luhmann-zettelkasten

   Investigate the scripts, JSON data, SQLite database,
   and network-analysis material.

   Treat this as a convenient data extraction and analysis
   project, not as an authoritative archival source.

RESEARCH REQUIREMENTS

A. RECONSTRUCT THE ACTUAL WORKFLOW

Determine how Luhmann:
- Captured fleeting ideas.
- Recorded observations and spontaneous thoughts.
- Processed reading notes.
- Recorded bibliographic information.
- Formulated permanent notes.
- Determined note granularity.
- Assigned note addresses.
- Created Folgezettel sequences.
- Established cross-references.
- Used indexes and retrieval mechanisms.
- Maintained and expanded the collection.
- Retrieved and combined notes when developing publications.

B. RECONSTRUCT THE INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Identify and distinguish:
- Note identifiers and addresses.
- Folgezettel sequences.
- Cross-references.
- Bibliographic records.
- Index entries.
- Topical relationships.
- Logical dependencies.
- Conceptual associations.
- Continuations and developments of existing arguments.

Do not assume that all these relationships represent
the same kind of connection.

C. DISTINGUISH FACT FROM INTERPRETATION

For every significant conclusion:
- Identify its source.
- Prefer direct archival evidence.
- Distinguish explicit instructions from observed practices.
- Distinguish Luhmann's actual methodology from modern
  Zettelkasten conventions.
- Identify ambiguities and conflicting interpretations.
- Do not invent procedures that the evidence does not support.

In particular, do not automatically impose the popular
modern three-stage workflow of fleeting notes, literature
notes, and permanent notes onto Luhmann's actual practices.

D. ANALYZE THE SYSTEM AS A WHOLE

Treat the Zettelkasten as both:
1. An information architecture.
2. A human intellectual workflow.

Determine which properties emerged from the physical
limitations of paper cards and which represented deliberate
intellectual or organizational design decisions.

Identify the essential principles that would remain valuable
in a modern digital implementation.

E. PRODUCE A COMPREHENSIVE TECHNICAL DESCRIPTION

Organize the research into:

1. Historical background and evolution.
2. Physical construction and organization.
3. Note-taking and note-processing workflows.
4. Note granularity and atomicity.
5. Addressing and Folgezettel.
6. Cross-referencing and relationships.
7. Bibliographic management.
8. Indexing and retrieval.
9. Maintenance and long-term evolution.
10. Luhmann's intellectual model of the system.
11. Evidence-supported workflow reconstruction.
12. Modern digital implementation considerations.
13. Common misconceptions about the Zettelkasten.

SOURCE HANDLING

- Preserve source URLs and bibliographic details.
- Cite archival note identifiers wherever possible.
- Preserve original German terminology alongside English
  translations.
- Do not silently substitute machine translations for
  published translations.
- Identify whether a source is primary or secondary.
- Record uncertainty rather than guessing.
- Prefer downloadable machine-readable source material.
- If a resource is inaccessible, report that limitation
  and continue with the remaining sources.

FINAL OBJECTIVE

Produce an evidence-based reconstruction of how Niklas
Luhmann's Zettelkasten actually worked.

The objective is NOT to explain how someone should build
a generic modern Zettelkasten.

The objective is to understand Luhmann's actual system,
its operational disciplines, its information architecture,
and the intellectual mechanisms that made it productive.
